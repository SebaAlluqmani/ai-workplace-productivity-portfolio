# Prompt Library — Project Coordinator

A collection of reusable, structured prompts built for recurring Project Coordinator tasks. Each prompt follows either the **C.A.R.E.** (Context, Action, Role, Expected Output) or **R.C.T.O.** (Role, Context, Task, Output Format) framework.

| Prompt Name | Workplace Task | Framework | Prompt | Expected Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deadline Shift Email | Client-facing schedule change | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Our 6-person team is midway through a client project and needs to shift the final deadline by two weeks. *Action:* Write an email to the client explaining the change and the new date. *Role:* Act as a calm, professional project coordinator. *Expected Output:* 150 words, 3 short paragraphs, professional tone, ends with a clear next step. | A short, plain email stating the delay, the reason in one line, the new date, and an offer to discuss further. |
| Director Status Brief | Summarizing 3 days of scattered updates for leadership | R.C.T.O. | *Role:* You are a project coordinator preparing a status brief. *Context:* You have three days of scattered project updates from email and chat. *Task:* Summarize the current state, flag open risks, and list the one decision still pending. *Output Format:* 5 bullet points, no more than 120 words total. | A tight bullet list separating "what happened," "what's still open," and "decision needed." |
| Messy Notes to Action Items | Turning meeting notes into a task table | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Here are unstructured notes from a stand-up meeting covering three days. *Action:* Extract every action item, owner, deadline, and priority into a table. *Role:* Act as a meticulous project coordinator. *Expected Output:* A Markdown table with columns Action Item, Owner, Deadline, Priority, Status — mark anything missing as "Not Specified." | A clean table with no invented owners or dates. |
| Same Message, Three Audiences | Adapting one update for team, manager, and client | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* The product rollout is delayed by two days due to a bug fix. *Action:* Write three versions of this update — for the internal team, for my direct manager, and for an external client. *Role:* Act as a professional, transparent project coordinator. *Expected Output:* Keep the facts identical across all three; vary only tone, length, and framing. | Three short messages with consistent facts and audience-appropriate tone. |
| Risk Escalation Note | Flagging a technical risk to leadership before a launch | R.C.T.O. | *Role:* You are a project coordinator escalating a risk. *Context:* Engineering flagged that server capacity might not hold at 100% load, and this hasn't been confirmed yet. *Task:* Write a short escalation message recommending we confirm capacity before committing to a launch date. *Output Format:* 4 sentences max, direct tone, no jargon. | A short, direct escalation that names the risk and the requested action, without alarmism. |
| Vendor Delay Notice | Informing an external vendor of a timeline change | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* A vendor's deliverable is now blocking our launch timeline by two days. *Action:* Write a message asking for an updated delivery estimate and flagging the impact on our schedule. *Role:* Act as a firm but courteous project coordinator. *Expected Output:* Under 100 words, polite but clear about the urgency. | Example output: "Hi [Vendor], our current timeline shows your deliverable landing two days later than planned, which is now our critical path to launch. Could you confirm a firm delivery date by tomorrow so we can adjust downstream planning if needed? Happy to hop on a call if that's faster. Thanks!" |

## Completion Notes
- All 6 prompts above are tied to the same Project Coordinator rollout scenario.
- Each prompt is tagged with its framework (C.A.R.E. or R.C.T.O.).
- Example outputs are included for the "Vendor Delay Notice" and "Deadline Shift Email" prompts (see `before-after-example.md` for the full before/after on the Deadline Shift Email).
