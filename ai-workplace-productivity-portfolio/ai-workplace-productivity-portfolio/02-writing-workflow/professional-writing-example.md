# Professional Writing Workflow — Client Update Email

Scenario: I asked an AI tool to draft a client email referencing quarterly performance data, then applied the 4-step drafting workflow (Draft → Verify → Refine Tone → Human Sign-off) before sending anything.

## 1. AI-Generated Draft
> "Hi [Client], great news — we're seeing strong momentum on the project, with a 23% increase reported last quarter. We expect this trend to continue and are on track for the next milestone. Let us know if you have any questions!"

## 2. Fact Verification
I checked the "23% increase" claim against our actual source report before sending anything.
- **Claim:** 23% increase reported last quarter.
- **Source checked:** Q2 internal performance report (spreadsheet, project drive).
- **Finding:** The real figure was **18%**, not 23%. The AI had generated a plausible-sounding but incorrect number.
- **Action taken:** Corrected the figure in the draft. Also checked the milestone date mentioned ("next milestone") against the project plan — confirmed accurate, no change needed.

## 3. Tone Refinement
The original draft was slightly too casual ("great news," exclamation marks) for a client who prefers a more measured, data-first tone. I adjusted the language to be confident but understated, removed the exclamation point, and moved the specific number earlier in the sentence for clarity.

## 4. Human Review / Sign-off
Final check: numbers verified against source, tone matched to this specific client's preferences, no invented claims remain. Approved and sent by me — the editor of record.

## 5. Final Version
> "Hi [Client], we wanted to share a quick update: last quarter's performance data shows an 18% increase, and the trend is holding steady heading into this quarter. We remain on track for the next milestone. Happy to walk through the numbers in more detail if useful."

---
**Key takeaway:** The AI's fluent, confident draft contained a fabricated statistic that would have gone to a client unverified if I hadn't checked it against the source. This is the core principle from the course: *smooth output is not necessarily correct output, and I remain the editor of record.*
