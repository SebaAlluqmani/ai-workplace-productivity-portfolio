# Personal Integration Plan — Project Coordinator

Three recurring tasks in my role where I plan to keep using generative AI, and how I'll do it responsibly.

## 1. Drafting Stakeholder Communications
- **Expected benefit:** Cuts first-draft time on routine emails (deadline changes, status updates) from ~20 minutes to ~5.
- **Preferred prompt/workflow:** C.A.R.E.-structured prompt, followed by the 4-step writing workflow (Draft → Verify → Refine Tone → Sign-off).
- **Key risk / verification step:** Never send a client-facing message without checking every number and date against the source document first.
- **Frequency of use:** Several times per week.
- **What success looks like:** Zero factual corrections needed after sending; consistent tone matched to each audience.

## 2. Triaging Scattered Updates into Status Briefs
- **Expected benefit:** Turns hours of scattered email/chat catch-up into a single decision-ready brief in under 15 minutes.
- **Preferred prompt/workflow:** R.C.T.O.-structured summarization prompt with an explicit instruction to mark unclear details as "Not Specified."
- **Key risk / verification step:** Cross-check the AI's summary against the original messages to confirm no detail was invented or dropped.
- **Frequency of use:** After any absence of a day or more, or before any leadership check-in.
- **What success looks like:** My director can act on the brief without needing to ask me a single clarifying question.

## 3. Building Rollout / Launch Plans Under Deadline Pressure
- **Expected benefit:** Converts a vague goal into a sequenced, assignable task list within minutes instead of an hour of manual planning.
- **Preferred prompt/workflow:** The Goal → Mechanisms → Phases → Tasks planning chain from Day 2.
- **Key risk / verification step:** Confirm that the plan's task order actually reflects real dependencies (e.g., don't book ads before the launch date is confirmed) — I don't accept the AI's sequencing without checking the logic myself.
- **Frequency of use:** Whenever a project hits a multi-decision bottleneck or a hard external deadline.
- **What success looks like:** A plan that the team can execute against immediately, with no step blocked by a decision that should have come earlier.
