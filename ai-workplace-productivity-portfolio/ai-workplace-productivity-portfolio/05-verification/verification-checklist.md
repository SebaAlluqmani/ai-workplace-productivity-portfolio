# Verification Checklist — Project Coordinator

A personal checklist I apply to any AI-generated content before it leaves my hands, built around the course's four-step protocol: Identify → Define Criteria → Test → Confirm & Decide.

## My Checklist
1. **List every specific claim** — numbers, dates, names, percentages, quotes — in the AI's output.
2. **For each claim, ask: is this something I actually know, or something the AI filled in?**
3. **Trace every number and date back to a real source document** (report, email thread, spreadsheet) — never to memory or "it sounds about right."
4. **Flag anything I can't verify** with "Not Specified" rather than letting it stay in the final text.
5. **Re-read the output as if I were the recipient** — would a wrong number here cause real harm (financial, legal, reputational)?
6. **Check tone against the actual audience**, not just grammar and spelling.
7. **Get a second pair of eyes on anything client-facing or high-stakes** before sending.
8. **Only after all of the above, sign off as the final, human-approved version.**

## Applied Example
Taken from the writing workflow example (Section 2): the AI draft claimed **"a 23% increase reported last quarter."**

- **Step 1 (List claims):** "23% increase" and "last quarter" are the two specific, checkable claims.
- **Step 2 (Define correct criteria):** The number must match the official Q2 performance report exactly.
- **Step 3 (Test against source):** Checked the actual Q2 internal performance report.
- **Step 4 (Confirm & decide):** Found the real figure was **18%**, not 23%. Corrected the draft before sending and documented the correction.

**Result:** Without this step, an incorrect statistic would have gone directly to a client. This is the exact behavior the course's "Verification & Human Accountability" principle is built around — generative AI is a fast drafter, not a fact-checked source of truth.
